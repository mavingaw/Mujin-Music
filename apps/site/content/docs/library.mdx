## Adding your music

During setup, choose the folder that contains your music and select **Use this folder**. After setup, add or refresh roots from **Settings → Library**. Each selected folder is registered independently.

You can select one or more:

- local folders and local drives;
- removable or external drives; and
- network folders already mounted and readable by the Windows account running Parson.

Parson reads files in place. It does not rename, move, or rewrite the source audio.

### When a folder is unavailable

An unavailable root fails that refresh, but Parson preserves the last known-good catalog from other roots—and the previous available catalog—rather than replacing everything with an empty library. Reconnect or remount the folder, then refresh it. Tracks on the offline root cannot play until the path is readable again.

## How Parson organizes music

Parson uses tags first, then bounded, deterministic inference when tags are incomplete or inconsistent. Folder names are evidence, not an instruction to mirror the disk layout.

### Artists and album artists

Track artists are the performers credited on a recording. Album artists own the release in the library. When album-artist tags are present, Parson uses them. When they are missing, it considers the artists shared across tracks in the same album folder and known aliases. Featuring credits stay attached to tracks without fragmenting the album artist unnecessarily.

### Credits, biographies and reviews

Composer, lyricist, arranger, conductor, performer (with instrument), ensemble, producer, remixer, mixer and engineer tags become credits. They appear on the album's **Credits** tab, under each track title ("Composed by …") and in **View credits…** on a track. Every name links to that person's **Credits** tab, which lists everything they composed, produced or played on in your library, filterable by role. People who only appear in credits get a contributor page instead of a discography.

**Online metadata** (Settings → Library, off by default) adds MusicBrainz relationship credits, release facts such as label and catalog number, Wikipedia biographies, album background and critical reception, and freely licensed artist photos from Wikimedia Commons. Wikipedia text always shows its article and CC BY-SA license; reviews are the article's reception section, not a paid review service. When MusicBrainz has two artists with the same name and nothing in your tags tells them apart, Parson shows no biography rather than a wrong one. You can also keep a private note on any album or artist.

### One person, every name

The same person is often credited under different names: tags may say "Q-Tip" where MusicBrainz credits "Jonathan Davis". With online metadata on, Parson learns the names MusicBrainz knows each person by (their own name, the names releases credit them as, and their aliases) and merges a tag credit into the MusicBrainz person by id or by one of those names. The album's Credits tab then lists them once ("Q-Tip, as Jonathan Davis"), their page collects every credit and album under both names, and a page opened under the other name moves to the merged one. A name two different MusicBrainz people share (Q-Tip and Korn's Jonathan Davis) is only merged on albums whose own MusicBrainz credits say who it is.

### Focus on a discography

Every person's **Discography** tab lists all their albums: the ones they made, conducted, appear on, composed for and produced. **Focus** narrows it with stackable filters: their own role, performer, conductor, ensemble, soloist, instrument, composer, form, period, bit depth, sample rate, quality (**Hi-Res only** means lossless at 24-bit or above 48 kHz) and source. Values in one filter widen the list and filters narrow it, so "Beethoven, conducted by Karajan, with the Philharmonia, 24-bit" is three choices on Beethoven's page, or "composer Beethoven, Philharmonia, Hi-Res only" on Karajan's. Each value shows how many albums choosing it would leave. A work's **Recordings** have the same filters. The choices stay in the page address, so a focused view can be bookmarked or shared. Ensembles are read from ensemble and orchestra tags, MusicBrainz credits, or a track artist named like an orchestra, choir or quartet. The source filter lists local files today; streaming albums join it once a streaming service adds them to the library.

### Works and recordings

With online metadata on, Parson also learns which **work** each track performs from MusicBrainz: the piece, its composer, form, key, catalogue number (Op. 125, BWV 1007, K. 550 …) and when it was composed, and which movement of the piece the track is. Movements of one piece are grouped under a heading in the album's track list. **Go to work** in a track's menu opens the work page, which lists every recording of it in your library with its conductor, orchestra and soloists, grouped by album, conductor or performer, and marks recordings that only include some movements. A composer's page has a **Compositions** tab grouped by form, with filter chips for form, period and key. When MusicBrainz links a piece to its Wikipedia article, the work page shows that article's introduction under **About**, and an album of the piece offers it as **About the work** next to the album's own review and background, so classical albums get text even when the release has no article.

### Genres

Parson reads genres from your files' tags (`GENRE` in FLAC and Ogg, `TCON` in MP3, `©gen` in MP4/M4A) and, with online metadata on, the genres MusicBrainz lists for the album's release group and for each artist. Every name is matched to a small genre tree with aliases, so "Bop" becomes Jazz › Bebop and "Hip-Hop", "hiphop" and "Rap" all mean Hip Hop; genres the tree does not know are kept as they are. Albums and artists show their genres as chips. Each chip opens a genre page with a description from Wikipedia (with its CC BY-SA attribution), its sub-genres and related genres, and the artists, composers and albums in your library in that genre or any genre below it. **Genres** in the sidebar lists the whole tree.

In **Settings → Online metadata**, **Genres from** chooses between online genres and file tags; each falls back to the other when an album has none. An administrator can choose an album's genres by hand with **Edit** next to its genre chips; that choice wins until it is reset.

Biographies, album background and reviews come straight from the Wikipedia article MusicBrainz links, or from the article whose Wikidata item MusicBrainz names; they do not wait for Wikidata, which is asked afterwards only for life dates, a photo and the article in your preferred language. An artist photo is the Wikidata image, the freely licensed Commons image MusicBrainz links, or the article's own Commons photo, in that order; photos without a free license (such as album covers uploaded under fair use) are never used. People MusicBrainz credits who have no tracks of their own in your library, such as producers, engineers and session players, are looked up by their MusicBrainz id too (up to 24 per album), so their contributor pages and credit avatars get the same biography and photo.

### Identify album

Albums whose tags do not name a MusicBrainz release are matched by title and album artist; classical titles such as "Beethoven: Symphony No. 9" also match without the composer, and the year in your tags picks between several recordings. When Parson finds no confident match, or the wrong one, an administrator can choose **Identify album…** in the album's menu, search MusicBrainz by title and artist, and pick the right release. That choice wins over tags and automatic matching until it is cleared. Placeholder names such as "Unknown Artist" or "Various Artists" are never matched automatically.

### Albums and editions

Albums are identified from normalized album title plus resolved album artist. Parson recognizes edition qualifiers such as deluxe, anniversary, remastered, expanded, special, collector's, limited, legacy, reissue, regional, mono/stereo, and bonus editions. It links confident variants into a release group while preserving the edition's visible title and tracks.

An original release and its deluxe edition remain separate albums. If an edition has no suitable cover, it may inherit the original release's artwork; its own good square cover always wins.

### Discs

Disc numbers order tracks inside one release. Folder or title suffixes such as `CD 1`, `Disc 2`, and `Disk 1 of 2` are understood as disc evidence, not automatically as separate albums. Release-level artwork can be found above a disc subfolder.

### Compilations

An album artist such as **Various Artists**, a compilation folder, or strong multi-artist evidence classifies a release as a compilation. Individual track artists remain searchable and visible.

### Singles, remixes, live releases, and bootlegs

Parson classifies releases using a combination of album title, folder hierarchy, track count, track-title markers, embedded genres, and album artist. Categories include Album, EP, Single, Remix, Compilation, Live, Demos & Rarities, Bootleg, Soundtrack, Promotional, Acapella, and Bonus Audio. A single weak word is not enough when the rest of the evidence disagrees.

Full-length, multi-song remix projects are shown as Remix Albums. Bundles of alternate versions of one lead recording are separated into Remix EPs and Remix Singles. DVD bonus-audio releases use the DVD Companion EP subtype, so content style does not obscure the release form.

### Loose tracks

A playable file still enters the library when its folder is not a conventional album layout. Its embedded tags provide the artist, album, title, track and disc numbers when available. Missing album context falls back conservatively rather than guessing a famous release.

### Missing or malformed tags

Parson normalizes Unicode, whitespace, dates, artist identities, track numbers, and common separators. Missing values fall back to safe labels such as **Unknown Artist**. Invalid dates and numbers are ignored. A file that cannot be parsed is skipped and recorded as a bounded scan warning; it does not fail the whole library.

## Avoiding accidental merges

Two recordings are treated as duplicates only when their release slot, normalized title, artist context, and duration agree closely. Editions stay distinct from originals. If two albums still merge or one album splits, correct the album/album-artist tags or use Parson's metadata editor, then rescan. See [Troubleshooting](/docs/troubleshooting#an-album-is-split).
